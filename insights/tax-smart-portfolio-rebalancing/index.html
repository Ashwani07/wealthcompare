Tax-Smart Portfolio Rebalancing: Realign Without Triggering Taxes

Published 30 October 2026 · 8 min read · Asset management and tax optimization guide for Indian investors

Every disciplined investor understands the textbook theory of asset allocation: define your target split (e.g. 70% Equity and 30% Debt), monitor portfolio drift, and rebalance when market rallies push your equity weight up to 80%.

However, when theoretical finance meets the Indian Income Tax Act, rebalancing by simply selling winners and buying losers triggers a painful penalty. Under Section 198 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (formerly Section 112A), equity gains above ₹1,25,000 are taxed at 12.5%. Debt mutual funds redeemed after April 2023 are taxed at your full income slab rate (up to 30% plus surcharge).

If you rebalance carelessly every 6 to 12 months by selling funds, you can end up losing 1.5% to 2.5% of your total compounded wealth directly to taxes and exit loads. Here is how Indian investors can execute tax-smart rebalancing.

1. The Drift Problem: Why Portfolios Naturally De-align

Suppose you build a ₹50,00,000 portfolio split 70% in Equity (₹35 Lakh) and 30% in Debt (₹15 Lakh). Over the next two years, the Indian stock market rallies 40%, while your debt funds return 7% per year.

Your equity holdings swell to ₹49 Lakh, while your debt grows to ₹17.2 Lakh. Your total portfolio is now ₹66.2 Lakh, and your equity weight has surged to 74.0%. Without taking any intentional action, your portfolio is now significantly riskier than you planned.

If you execute this the traditional way by selling ₹2.66 Lakh of equity to purchase debt, you realize capital gains. If your cost basis was ₹1.9 Lakh, you generate ₹76,000 in capital gains. While this specific trim falls within the ₹1.25 Lakh annual Section 198 exemption, a larger ₹1 Crore+ portfolio rebalance can easily trigger ₹50,000 to ₹1,00,000 in unnecessary tax outgo.

2. Strategy 1: Cash-Flow Inflow Rebalancing (Zero Tax)

The cleanest, 100% tax-free method to rebalance is Inflow Rebalancing. Instead of selling existing equity units, use your monthly SIP contributions and annual corporate bonuses to purchase the underweight asset class.

In our example above, Debt is underweight by ₹2.66 Lakh. If your household invests ₹60,000 per month across SIPs, redirect 100% of your upcoming monthly SIPs into your debt funds for 4.5 months. Your equity weight naturally drops back to 70% as the portfolio base grows, without selling a single equity unit, without paying a single rupee of LTCG tax, and without incurring mutual fund exit loads.

3. Strategy 2: The ±5% Tolerance Band Rule

Never rebalance based on the calendar (e.g., "rebalance every 1st of January"). Rebalancing on a fixed calendar date generates excessive churn during mild sideways markets.

Instead, use Tolerance Bands (Opportunistic Rebalancing). Set a ±5% deviation threshold:

  • If your target equity is 70%, take no action as long as equity stays between 65% and 75%.
  • Only trigger a rebalancing intervention when equity breaks above 75% or drops below 65%.

Empirical market studies prove that tolerance band rebalancing captures most of the risk-reduction benefits of rebalancing while reducing lifetime transaction friction by over 60%.

4. Strategy 3: Tax-Loss & Section 198 Exemption Harvesting

When portfolio drift is so large that fresh cash flows cannot fix it within 6 months, selling becomes necessary. When selling, apply these three tax-smart rules:

  1. Harvest the ₹1,25,000 Section 198 Exemption: Section 198 provides an annual tax-free window for equity LTCG. If you must trim equity, sell lots that generate up to ₹1,25,000 of gains per financial year. If rebalancing in February or March, split your sales across March 31 and April 1 to utilize two separate financial years of exemptions (₹2,50,000 total tax-free gains).
  2. Avoid Redeeming Non-Equity Debt Funds: Since the Finance Act 2023 amendment, specified debt funds are taxed at your slab rate without indexation. When trimming debt, always redeem high-yield savings FDs, arbitrage funds, or equity savings funds first rather than pure debt funds.
  3. Set Off Short-Term Capital Losses: If you hold any individual stocks or thematic mutual fund units trading at a loss, booking those losses can set off short-term capital gains (STCG) or long-term capital gains (LTCG), wiping out your tax liability.

5. The 4-Step Rebalancing Protocol

1. Check portfolio weights every 6 months against your target.
2. Did any asset class drift by > ±5%? If NO, do nothing.
3. If YES, can 3–6 months of fresh SIP cashflow restore balance? If YES, reroute SIPs.
4. If NO, trim the overweight asset class using the ₹1.25L Section 198 exemption limit.
