Why the 10x Income Rule Fails: How to Calculate Exact Term Cover

Published 30 October 2026 · 8 min read · Underwriting and HLV guidelines for Indian policyholders

Whenever an Indian salaried professional looks to buy life insurance, insurance web portals and bank relationship managers repeat the exact same heuristic: "Just take 10 times your annual CTC."

If you earn ₹25,00,000 per year, they sell you a ₹2.5 Crore policy. If you earn ₹15,00,000, they hand you a ₹1.5 Crore policy. It sounds simple, clean, and intuitive. It is also financially dangerous.

The "10x annual income" rule completely ignores your family's actual liabilities, outstanding home loans, number of children, and existing liquid assets. In many common Indian household situations, following the 10x rule leaves a surviving spouse and young children under-insured by 50% or more, while in others it forces people to pay premiums for coverage they do not need.

1. The Critical Flaws of the 10x Heuristic

The 10x income heuristic fails because it assumes that two families earning identical incomes have identical financial obligations. Consider two 38-year-old professionals, both earning ₹25 Lakh per year:

  • Person A: Has an outstanding ₹80 Lakh home loan in Bengaluru, two school-going children aged 4 and 7 whose college degrees will cost ₹1.2 Crore after inflation, and an elderly dependent mother.
  • Person B: Lives in an inherited, debt-free house in Chandigarh, has one child entering final year of college, and holds ₹1.5 Crore in mutual funds and EPF.

The 10x rule prescribes the exact same ₹2.5 Crore policy to both. For Person B, ₹2.5 Crore is more than adequate. For Person A, if the primary breadwinner passes away tomorrow, clearing the ₹80 Lakh home loan immediately consumes nearly a third of the payout, leaving just ₹1.7 Crore. After funding college milestones, the remaining sum will not generate enough interest to sustain monthly grocery and utility bills for 10 years.

2. The Needs-Based Human Life Value (HLV) Method

Professional fee-only financial planners use the Needs-Based HLV Method, which views term insurance as an exact liability-matching mechanism. The formula evaluates four distinct liabilities and subtracts existing family resources:

Gross Family Need =
  1. Income Replacement (Living expenses until retirement age)
+ 2. Debt Clearance (Home loan + Car loan + Personal loans)
+ 3. Future Milestone Goals (Child school, college, higher degrees at 6% inflation)
+ 4. Final Expenses & Transition Buffer (Emergency cash for probate and grief)

Additional Term Cover Needed =
Gross Family Need - Usable Liquid Assets - Existing Life Cover

The 10x rule suggested ₹2.64 Crore. The family's real financial requirement is ₹5.25 Crore. A shortfall of ₹2.61 Crore would have left the surviving family in severe distress.

4. Three Costly Term Insurance Traps to Avoid

Trap 1: Term with Return of Premium (TROP)

Insurance distributors heavily pitch TROP plans with the line: "Why waste your premium? If you survive to age 60, we give you 100% of your money back."

What they conceal is that a ₹2 Crore pure term plan costs roughly ₹20,000 per year, while the identical TROP plan costs ₹55,000 per year. Over 25 years, you pay an extra ₹35,000 annually. At age 60, the insurer refunds your ₹13.75 Lakh of nominal payments. If you had simply invested that ₹35,000 difference in a basic Nifty 50 Index Fund at 11% CAGR, you would have accumulated over ₹40 Lakh.

Trap 2: Whole Life Cover up to Age 85 or 99

Life insurance is designed to replace lost future earnings during your working years. By age 60 or 65, your home loan is repaid, your children are earning, and your retirement corpus is accumulated. Paying 2x higher premiums for whole-life term cover is unnecessary: you do not need life insurance when nobody depends on your monthly salary.

Trap 3: Limited Pay (Pay for 5 or 10 Years)

Limited pay plans compress 30 years of premiums into 5 or 10 years. Unless you are a professional athlete or merchant navy worker with a very short earning window, regular annual pay is superior. If you reach financial independence early at age 52, you can simply cancel a regular pay policy and stop paying premiums.

5. The IRDAI Underwriting Checklist

  1. Section 45 of the Insurance Act, 1938: After a life insurance policy has been in force for three consecutive years, no insurer can reject a claim on grounds of non-disclosure or misstatement. Fill the proposal form yourself with 100% honesty regarding smoking, alcohol, and family medical history.
  2. Amount Settlement Ratio (ASR): Do not look only at Claim Settlement Ratio (CSR). An insurer can have a 99% CSR by settling millions of tiny ₹50,000 endowment claims while scrutinizing and rejecting ₹2 Crore term claims. Check IRDAI’s Amount Settlement Ratio in their annual report.
  3. Opt for the Married Women’s Property (MWP) Act: When buying the policy, select the MWP Act endorsement (Section 6). This legally earmarks the claim proceeds exclusively for your wife and children, protecting the payout from business creditors, courts, and lender attachments.

Regulatory and Statutory References

  • Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI): Annual Report and Claim Settlement Statistics. Verified at irdai.gov.in.
  • Section 45 of the Insurance Act, 1938: Policy cannot be called in question after three years.
  • Section 6 of the Married Women’s Property Act, 1874: Protection of policy proceeds for beneficiaries.
