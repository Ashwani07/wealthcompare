The Growing Annuity Due Model

Unlike simplistic calculators that multiply annual expense by 25 or 30, this model computes the exact present value of an inflation-indexed growing annuity due. Withdrawals occur at the start of each year, and remaining capital compounds at net post-retirement returns (postRetReturn - postRetDrag).

Taxation at Retirement (LTCG)

Pre-retirement accumulation estimates capital gains by subtracting your total contributions (initial corpus plus SIPs) and the Section 198 exemption (₹1.25 lakh). The 12.5% LTCG tax is deducted to reflect your true net available starting corpus at age 60.

Sequence of Returns Risk

The mathematical schedule assumes an even annual return of 7% net post-retirement. In real markets, experiencing a 20–30% drawdown in your first 3 retirement years while actively withdrawing money permanently shrinks capital longevity. It is critical to maintain a 2–3 year cash buffer during retirement.

Planning Retirement in India: The Real Math

Retirement planning in India faces a dual challenge that western models like the famous "4% Rule" overlook: persistently high inflation (6–7%) paired with longer post-retirement lifespans (25–35 years).

1. The Compounding Trap of Inflation

A monthly lifestyle that costs ₹1,00,000 today will require approximately ₹3,20,700 per month in 20 years at 6% inflation. If your retirement lasts 30 years (from age 60 to 90), that monthly outgo will cross ₹18,00,000 by age 89 just to sustain the exact same purchasing power.

2. Why Pure Fixed Deposits Fail in Retirement

Many retirees move 100% of their savings into Bank FDs or Senior Citizens Savings Schemes (SCSS). When post-tax FD interest (typically 5.2%–6.0% for 30% slab earners) is lower than lifestyle inflation (6%–7%), the real purchasing power of the corpus shrinks every single year. A sustainable retirement portfolio requires a balanced asset allocation (30–40% equity, 60–70% debt and arbitrage) to beat inflation.
