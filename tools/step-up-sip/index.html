The goal

The cost you enter is in today's rupees. It is multiplied by (1 + inflation) for each year to get the amount you will need in the future. Anything you have already invested is grown at your assumed return and counts towards that amount.

The starting SIP

Each year's SIP is the previous year's multiplied by (1 + step-up), paid in twelve equal month-end instalments. Each instalment grows at the yearly return divided by 12, compounded monthly, until the goal date. The starting SIP is the one amount that makes the money after tax equal to the future cost of the goal. We solve it directly with algebra, so it is exact, not a guess.

Tax

Tax is worked out once, at the end, as if you sold everything. It is 12.5% of the gain above ₹1,25,000 (Section 198 of the Income-tax Act, 2025). The money you already have invested counts as part of what you paid in. This treats every rupee of gain as long-term. Units held for 12 months or less are taxed at 20% (Section 196), so a very short plan will pay more than shown. Cess, surcharge, exit loads and fund costs are not included.

Today's rupees

To compare what you pay in with the goal fairly, each year's instalments are divided by (1 + inflation) for that year, the same way our other tools do it. The final-year SIP is shown as "about ₹X in today's money" by dividing by (1 + inflation) for the years that have passed before it starts.

What is not included

Missed or changed instalments, a pay rise that doesn't match your step-up, and market ups and downs are not modelled. Real results will differ, which is why the lower-return table is worth a look. Our article on what a SIP return assumption really means explains why.

How the Step-Up SIP Goal calculation works

A step-up SIP (also known as a top-up SIP) allows you to automatically increase your monthly mutual fund investment by a set percentage each year. This reflects the reality of household finances: as your career progresses and your earnings increase, your capacity to save and invest grows along with it.

Why start with a step-up rather than a flat SIP?

Targeting a large future milestone (such as a child's higher education or retirement) with a fixed monthly SIP often yields an intimidatingly large starting amount. For example, accumulating a corpus equivalent to ₹1 crore in today's money over 20 years with a flat SIP might require ₹25,000 to ₹30,000 per month right from month one.

With an annual 10% step-up, you can start with a significantly smaller monthly amount (e.g. ₹11,000 to ₹13,000). As your salary grows with annual increments, the stepped-up instalments absorb a manageable share of your incremental income.

How the calculator solves for your starting SIP

Most basic calculators ask you for your starting amount and tell you where you end up. This calculator reverses the problem to solve directly for the monthly amount you need to invest today:

  • Step 1 (Inflate the goal): Your goal is expressed in today's purchasing power. We first calculate the future target corpus: Future Goal = Goal in Today's Money × (1 + inflation)years.
  • Step 2 (Existing corpus growth): Any money already invested in mutual funds is projected forward at your expected return rate to reduce the burden on your monthly SIP.
  • Step 3 (Solve the step-up annuity with tax): The calculator solves algebraically for the exact starting monthly contribution, accounting for compounding, annual step-up jumps, and the 12.5% Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax on equity fund profits above ₹1.25 lakh under Section 198 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Choosing a realistic step-up percentage

While a 15% or 20% annual step-up makes the required starting SIP look delightfully small, it can become burdensome later. If you start with ₹15,000 and step up 15% each year, by Year 15 your monthly contribution will exceed ₹1,05,000 per month.

For most salaried Indian households, an annual step-up between 5% and 10% matches realistic long-term career salary growth without creating cash-flow strain in later years.
