Path A: Invest the surplus in equity

Normal loan EMI is paid on schedule every month. The surplus (lump sum and monthly extra) is invested in diversified mutual funds compounding at (grossReturn - expenseRatio). Capital gains tax (12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh under Section 198) is deducted at the end of tenure.

Path B: Prepay loan & reduce tenure

Surplus funds directly reduce loan principal. When the loan closes early, the entire former EMI plus surplus is invested into mutual funds for the remaining months so both paths commit identical cash.

Path C: Prepay loan & reduce EMI

Prepayments lower monthly EMI across remaining tenure. The monthly cashflow difference is invested into mutual funds every single month.

Hurdle rate formula

Gross Hurdle Return = [Loan Rate ÷ (1 - LTCG Tax)] + Expense Ratio. If your expected mutual fund return does not exceed this rate, prepaying is guaranteed to build more wealth.

Prepaying vs Investing: The Core Principles

When you have surplus cash flow, deciding between prepaying your home loan and investing in equity mutual funds requires balancing guaranteed interest savings against long-term market compounding.

Every rupee used to prepay a 7.5% loan gives an immediate, 100% risk-free return of 7.5% after tax. To beat that in mutual funds, after accounting for 12.5% Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax and fund management fees, your portfolio must achieve a gross return higher than your hurdle rate.

Key trade-offs to keep in mind:

  • Liquidity: Money paid to a lender is locked into property equity and cannot be accessed in an emergency without taking another loan. Mutual funds can be redeemed in two working days.
  • Sequence of returns risk: Stock market returns arrive erratically. A bear market right before your loan ends can erase projected investment gains. Loan interest, however, compounds with mathematical certainty.
  • The 50/50 approach: If you are torn between peace of mind and mathematical growth, consider directing 50% of surplus to prepayments and 50% to an equity SIP.
