Tax-Smart Rebalancing Rules in India

Rebalance With Fresh SIP Inflows First

Instead of selling debt to buy equity (or vice versa), which triggers capital gains tax and transaction fees, direct your monthly SIP contributions exclusively into the underweight asset class until the portfolio realigns naturally.

Section 198 Tax Exemption Harvesting

If you must sell equity to trim an overweight position, remember that equity LTCG is taxed at 12.5% on gains above ₹1,25,000 per financial year. Liquidating lots with minimal gains or spacing redemptions across March and April utilizes two financial years of exemptions.

EPF and PPF Are Illiquid Debt

Remember that Employee Provident Fund (EPF) and Public Provident Fund (PPF) cannot be easily withdrawn before retirement or lock-in. When rebalancing out of debt, trim liquid debt mutual funds or bank fixed deposits rather than retirement accounts.

Why Asset Allocation Drives 90% of Portfolio Returns

Decades of academic finance research (such as the Brinson, Hood, and Beebower studies) confirm that over 90% of portfolio performance variability comes from asset allocation—the split between Equity, Debt, Gold, and Cash—rather than individual stock picking or mutual fund timing.

1. The Mechanical Advantage of Rebalancing

During a roaring bull market, your equity allocation will expand naturally from 60% to 75% as stock prices rise. If you do nothing, your portfolio becomes dangerously volatile right before a correction. Rebalancing forces you to do what human psychology resists: sell high and buy low.

2. The Role of Uncorrelated Assets (Gold and Cash)

Allocating 5% to 10% in Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) or Gold ETFs provides an essential macroeconomic hedge during global currency debasement or geopolitical crises. Maintaining a 5% liquid cash allocation ensures you can rebalance into equities during market crashes without disturbing your emergency reserves.
