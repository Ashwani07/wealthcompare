The growth

Your yearly return is divided by 12 and applied to the balance every month. Each instalment goes in at the end of its month. A lump sum at the start grows at the same monthly rate for the whole period. The same rate applies in every month, which no real market does. See how that changes things in our article on what a SIP return assumption really means.

Today's rupees

A future amount is divided by (1 + inflation) for each year that passes. Instalments paid later are in cheaper rupees, so the money you paid in is also brought back to today's value, year by year, before it is compared with the final amount. That is why the "real gain" here is measured against what you paid in, in today's money, not against the plain total.

Tax

Tax is worked out once, at the end, as if you sold everything. It is 12.5% of the gain above ₹1,25,000 (Section 198 of the Income-tax Act, 2025). This treats every rupee of gain as long-term. In practice, units held for 12 months or less are taxed at 20% (Section 196), so a very short SIP will pay more than shown. Cess, surcharge, exit loads and fund costs are not included. Check the Income Tax Department's pages for current rules.

What is not included

Missed or changed instalments, a rising SIP amount, and market ups and downs are not modelled. Real results will differ.
