Advanced Read: how these numbers are worked out

Month by month

Each month, interest is the balance at the start of the month multiplied by the yearly rate divided by 12. Your EMI is paid first. Any extra payment or lump sum due that month is then paid against the remaining balance. A prepayment can never be more than what is still owed.

Reduce EMI

After a month with a prepayment, the EMI is recalculated for the balance and the months still left on the original schedule. The loan ends close to the original date, but each instalment is smaller.

Reduce Tenure

The EMI stays where it is. Because the balance falls faster, the loan ends earlier. For the same money prepaid at the same time, this normally saves more interest than lowering the EMI.

Rate change

Keep EMI, change tenure: the instalment stays the same, so a lower rate shortens the loan and a higher rate lengthens it. Keep tenure, recalculate EMI: the EMI is recalculated at the new rate for the months left. If a prepayment was made the month before the rate change and you chose Reduce EMI, the new EMI is first worked out at the old rate and then the new rate applies from the change month.

What is not included

Prepayment charges, processing fees, insurance, tax benefits, missed payments and any lender-specific day-count rules are not included. All amounts are actual rupees paid in the future; no inflation adjustment is applied. If a rate rise is large enough that the EMI no longer covers the interest, the page tells you the loan is not repaid within 40 years.
